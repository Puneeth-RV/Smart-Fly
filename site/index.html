Smart-Fly

A fruit fly’s brain, learning to play Mario.

The eye and visual system below are simulated neuron by neuron from the fruit-fly connectome, the complete wiring diagram of a real fly brain. It sees only the game’s pixels. A small learned network turns what those neurons do into button presses, and practice is the only teacher: moving forward is rewarded, dying is punished.

These are recorded runs. The fly keeps training on a laptop; the recordings and the numbers below are refreshed from time to time.

      How it sees

      Left to right is the path every frame takes, about fifteen times a second.

      1. 1 What reaches the eye

        The game reduced to the 24 × 15 patches the eye covers. Bright marks are edges.

      2. 2 Photoreceptors

        The 3,354 light-sensing neurons themselves, responding. This is the brain’s own activity, not the picture.

      3. 3 Deeper optic lobe

        The rest of the visual system. The picture blurs here; the wiring is real, the connection strengths are not known.

      4. 4 What decided it

        Which parts of its view moved this choice most. Ideally the next obstacle.

      480 optic-lobe neurons over the last few seconds, top of the view to bottom. Each mark is a neuron strongly active at that moment.

      The whole game

      It moves on once it reaches the flag in at least 8 of 10 test runs, each starting slightly differently, choosing only its top button. Levels it can’t pass yet are set aside and revisited.
